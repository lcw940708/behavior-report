業主大會吐苦水 灑向居民都是怨 - 東方日報

發佈時間：Thu, 03 Sep 2026 19:29:28 GMT
Title: 業主大會怒吐水，怨恨未交付樓隨升降機保養費（East Day Post） Business owners at the Big Buck Housing Estate in Sham Shui Po expressed their grievances towards the residents during a meeting, as the soft and hard measures from the government had made the property acquisition process remarkably smooth. Out of the eight buildings with a total of 1,984 households, 1,971 households, or 99.3%, had signed and returned the "Acceptance of Purchase Recommendation Letter," while 13 households, known as the "hard nuts," refused to sell. Even in the case of the unscathed Marco Chi Building, over three-quarters of the owners had already signed the "Sale and Purchase Agreement." However, the maintenance of the elevators in the Marco Chi Building, which should be the responsibility of the third party, had become a source of contention. The latest contract was about to expire, and the question of who would pay the fees and why the maintenance was still required, aggravated the situation. The manager of Mei Keung Management Co. Limited held a special meeting for the estate residents on February 2, 2023, to discuss the status and progress of various service contracts, including the new three-year elevator maintenance contracts for Marco Chi Building that started on March 16, 2023. The current contract was set to expire on June 16, 2023, and the costs were to be covered by a charitable organization's donations. Despite the temporary third-party funding for the elevator maintenance, the elevators were largely idle, as residents were still unable to move back into their homes, and the government continued to pressure them to sell their property rights. The residents complained that they were being forced to pay for the elevator maintenance while they were not even allowed to use their apartments, which they found unreasonable. The measuring engineer argued that the elevator maintenance was necessary to enable residents to retrieve their belongings from their apartments, making it a reasonable requirement. However, the intended use of the charitable organization's donations and their original purpose was unclear, raising concerns that the funds might be diverted to other parts of the estate. Furthermore, the estate's tenancy agreement, signed by all property owners, stipulated that if the donations ceased, all residents would have to share the costs, potentially sparking dissent among the other seven buildings' residents. The engineer also highlighted that the tenants' feelings were easily hurt, as the proposed reduction in the government's purchase price was less attractive than before, and the residents were required to pay stamp duty. For elderly tenants on fixed incomes, this was a significant burden, forcing them to downsize their living conditions in their twilight years. From the government's perspective, the swift resolution of the property acquisition process was crucial. Nevertheless, the handling of the fire disaster in late 2022 was perceived as less than ideal by the majority of the public, with the government's approach raising questions about transparency and fairness. Key Points: 1. Business owners at Big Buck Housing Estate expressed grievances towards residents over elevator maintenance fees. 2. The government's soft and hard measures made the property acquisition process smooth, but residents were unable to move back into their apartments. 3. Concerns over the intended use of donations for elevator maintenance and the potential impact on other buildings' residents.
