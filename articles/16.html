Title: Rescue Success in Tibetan Landslide | Two Workers Saved from Upper Trishuli 3A Hydroelectric Power Station Date: 05:56, September 5, 2026 (HKT) Background and Core Events: A landslide caused by a torrential monsoon in late July 2026 along the border of Nepal and China's Tibet Autonomous Region buried several hydropower stations with rocks, mud, and gravel. On the ninth day of the rescue operation, a team saved two workers from the Upper Trishuli 3A Hydroelectric Power Station, marking the first rescued personnel in the tunnel search and rescue efforts for this hydropower project. Key Details and Data: The rescue team, comprised of experts, employed a combination of engineering blueprints, satellite data, remote expert guidance, piped oxygen, and on-site digging and searching. The team's method, which had not been seen before on a global scale, proved effective but stressed the importance of considering local geological conditions and tunnel structures. Rescued Workers: The rescued workers were a 30-year-old mechanical engineer, Sanjay Sah, and a mechanical supervisor, Kabir Maharjan. Nepalese authorities released footage showing rescue personnel pulling Maharjan from a subterranean shaft, followed by applause. Sah was carried out of the tunnel by the rescue team, still covered in mud, and was then fitted with an oxygen mask for examination. Summary and Conclusion: - A devastating landslide in late July 2026 along the Nepal-Tibet border buried several hydropower stations. - The rescue team successfully saved two workers on the ninth day of the operation using innovative methods. - The team's success underscores the importance of considering local geological conditions and tunnel structures when applying similar rescue strategies. Bullet Points: - A landslide in July 2026 buried several hydropower stations along the Nepal-Tibet border. - The rescue team, using innovative methods, saved two workers from the Upper Trishuli 3A Hydroelectric Power Station on the ninth day of the operation. - The team's success emphasizes the importance of considering local geological conditions and tunnel structures when implementing rescue strategies.
