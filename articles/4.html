發佈時間：Thu, 03 Sep 2026 19:29:28 GMT
Title: 業主大會抱怨：保養升降機費用抑或是洗巧子？ - on.cc東網 Business owners at the Macau Park Estate have signed agreements to accept the government's buyout offers, with 99.3% of the 1,984 residents having complied. However, the issue of maintaining the elevator maintenance for the Marco Chi Building, which was not affected by the fire, has sparked controversy. The maintenance fee for the elevator needs to be paid every three months and the latest contract is about to expire on June 15. The government has agreed to pay for the maintenance through charitable donations. Yet, the question of who should bear the cost for future maintenance and why it is necessary has led to disagreements among residents. Elevator maintenance is unnecessary The management company, Anchor Management, held a special meeting for residents on June 2 to discuss the status and progress of various service contracts, including the new three-year elevator maintenance contract for Marco Chi Building starting from March 16. With the current contract ending on June 16, and the donated funds being used to pay for it, the payment arrangement has raised concerns. Unnecessary expense or a cover-up? The measurement expert argues that maintaining the elevator is necessary for residents to retrieve their belongings and is a reasonable practice. However, the use of charitable donations for this purpose and the intended purpose of those donations is unknown. There is a possibility that a portion of the funds could be redirected to the maintenance of other buildings in the estate. Burden on all residents As the Macau Park Estate property agreement is a collective agreement among all property owners, should donations cease, all residents may be required to share the cost or face backlash from other residents in the seven other buildings. Money talks, feelings get hurt The possibility of future reduced compensation for property owners raises questions about the government's motives and whether it might further pressure the remaining 13 residents of Marco Chi Building to sell their properties quickly. With the elevator operating normally and the building's structural integrity intact, residents question why they are still required to pay for its maintenance and why they are not allowed to use it freely. Unfair treatment and lack of transparency The government's inconsistent handling of the situation has led to suspicion and frustration among residents. While some residents have been granted permission to move back into their apartments, others have been left in the dark about the reason for the selective treatment. The lack of clear communication from the government has added to the unease among residents. Key Points: 1. The controversy over maintaining the elevator in the Marco Chi Building, which was not affected by the fire, has led to disagreements among residents. 2. The government has agreed to pay for the maintenance through charitable donations, but the intended use of those donations is unknown. 3. The possibility of reduced compensation for property owners may pressure them to sell their properties quickly and fuel dissatisfaction among residents.
